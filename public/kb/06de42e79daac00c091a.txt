pinworm infection, also known as enterobiasis, is a common infection that is not serious. it is caused by small white worms in the intestine and often causes no symptoms. with a new infection, small worms may be visible in the stool (poop), and itching may occur around the anus, especially at night. what you can do at home what to do ask a pharmacist about treatment for the infected person and anyone who lives with them or has had close contact with them. everyone should start treatment at the same time. repeat the treatment after 2 weeks. follow the pharmacist's treatment instructions. if the area is itchy, ask a pharmacist for advice before applying an ointment, cream, or lotion. on the first day of treatment wash bedding, towels, and washcloths in hot water. vacuum the bedrooms. to prevent spreading the infection wash hands often, especially after using the toilet and before eating. keep nails short and clean. avoid putting fingers in the mouth, biting nails, or scratching around the anus. clean the toilet seat thoroughly. take a shower every day, preferably in the morning. change pyjamas every day. change underwear every day and always put on clean underwear after showering. do not shake clothing or bedding, as this may spread the eggs. regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects, such as toys. for babies and young children: use mittens or close-fitting underwear to prevent scratching. à éviter baths as the water may contain pinworm eggs. when to seek medical attention ask a pharmacist whether treatment is needed a child under 2 years of age has symptoms or lives with someone who has a pinworm infection a pregnant or breastfeeding person has symptoms or lives with someone who has a pinworm infection. symptoms are still present after the second treatment severe itching around the anus or genital area lasts more than a week despite following the recommended advice the skin around the scratched area becomes red or swollen need help? call info-santé at 811, option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time. go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment. make an appointment at your medical clinic or clsc. if you do not have a family doctor, go to gap.soinsvirtuels or call 811, option 3.
