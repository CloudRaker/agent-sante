when the skin comes into contact with something very hot, such as fire, steam, boiling liquid, or a hot surface. water-filled blisters may also appear. what you can do at home official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/heat-related-burn/ what to do cool the burn immediately by running cool (not ice-cold) water over it for at least 20 minutes. remove clothing and jewelry near the burn, unless they are stuck to the skin. gently wash the burn with tap water and mild, unscented soap. if the leg or arm is burned, keep that part of the body slightly elevated as often as possible for 1 to 2 days to help prevent swelling. to reduce pain, take acetaminophen (tylenol) or ibuprofen (advil). when in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice. important: ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age. if there is no open wound and the blister has not popped leave the burn uncovered. apply an unscented moisturizer or aloe vera gel. keep the burn clean. if there is an open wound, or if the blister has popped apply an antibiotic ointment (e.g., polysporin), unless contraindicated. when in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice. cover the burn with a dressing and change it once a day or when it becomes dirty. wash the burn before applying a new dressing. what to avoid do not apply butter, toothpaste, or other greasy products. do not pop water blisters (also called blisters). when to seek medical attention official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/heat-related-burn/ a water blister (blister) larger than 2.5 cm pain that continues even after taking medication a burn that has a bad smell, or redness, swelling, or warmth around the burn, or pus (yellow-green fluid) fever a burn that has not healed after 14 days, even after following the recommended care difficulty breathing, coughing, a hoarse voice, or black mucus when coughing it is important to check whether the burned person's tetanus vaccination is up to date. need help? call
