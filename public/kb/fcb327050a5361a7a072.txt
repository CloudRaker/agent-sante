reduces hospital admissions related to rsv. preventive treatment against rsv infections official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/immunization-against-respiratory-syncytial-rsv-infections/babies preventive treatment provides the baby with antibodies that help fight rsv and prevent complications from the infection. the treatment is given by injection and protects the baby for at least 5 months. babies eligible for protection against rsv free of charge official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/immunization-against-respiratory-syncytial-rsv-infections/babies protection is offered free of charge during the rsv immunization campaign (generally in fall and winter) for: newborns born during the rsv season (generally in fall and winter); babies born on or after april 1, 2026 who have not already received it; babies born on or after february 1, 2026 who have not received it and who are more at risk of complications, namely: babies who were born at less than 37 weeks of pregnancy; babies who have at least one of the conditions that increase the risk of complications; babies who were born on or after march 1, 2025 who have at least one of the conditions that increase the risk of complications. they could also receive a 2nd dose in their 2nd rsv season. if in doubt, a health professional can help you determine if your child is concerned. conditions that increase the risk of complications from rsv official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/immunization-against-respiratory-syncytial-rsv-infections/babies certain conditions increase the risk of complications from rsv, such as: being born at less than 32 weeks of pregnancy; cystic fibrosis; trisomy 21 (down syndrome); lung diseases: bronchopulmonary dysplasia, chronic lung disease or moderate or severe pulmonary arterial hypertension; respiratory problems caused by a muscular disorder or malformation: significant impaired clearance of secretions from the airways due to a neuromuscular disorder or a congenital anomaly of the upper airways; disease or malformation of the heart: congenital heart disease or hemodynamically significant cardiomyopathy; bone marrow, stem cell or solid organ transplant. reactions that may occur after preventive treatment official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/immunization-against-respiratory-syncytial-rsv-infections/babies preventive treatment against rsv is safe. most of the time, it does not cause any reaction. nature and frequency of reactions that may occur after preventive treatment frequency reactions that may occur after preventive treatment sometimes (less than 1% of people) pain, redness or swelling at the injection site other health problems, such as a cold or gastroenteritis, may arise by chance after administration, but are not caused by preventive treatment. what to do after preventive treatment is administered official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/immunization-against-respiratory-syncytial-rsv-infections/babies instructions to follow after preventive treatment is administered wait 15 minutes before leaving the premises. if an allergic reaction occurs, the symptoms will appear within a few minutes of administration of preventive treatment. if symptoms appear, inform the health professional immediately, who will be able to treat the reaction on site. advice to follow at home if there is redness, pain or swelling at the injection site, apply a cold wet compress to the injection site. use medication for fever or discomfort as needed (such as acetaminophen, e.g., tylenol® or tempra®). when to consult official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/vaccination/immunization-against-respiratory-syncytial-rsv-infections/babies consult a health professional in any of the following situations: if there are serious or unusual symptoms;
