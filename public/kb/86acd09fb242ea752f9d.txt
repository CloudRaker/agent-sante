highly contagious viral illness. red spots or bumps appear on the skin, followed by fluid-filled blisters that eventually form scabs. the skin may be very itchy. what you can do at home official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/chickenpox/ what to do take acetaminophen (tylenol). when in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice. caring for the spots take a 10-minute lukewarm bath once a day, as needed. use a mild, unscented soap and rinse thoroughly. pat the skin dry. do not rub. do not remove firmly attached scabs. remove only loose scabs that catch onto clothing. moisturize the skin after a bath or shower, for example with a lanolin-based cream, petroleum jelly, or mineral oil, unless this makes the rash worse. important: never leave a child unattended in the bath. pain when urinating urinate in the bath or a sitz bath, or while warm water runs over the painful area. itchy skin wear loose, soft clothing. if the skin is not dry, add baking soda to the bathwater: 50 to 60 ml for children under 2 years of age, or 250 to 500 ml for adults and children aged 2 years and older. apply cold, wet compresses to the skin for 30 to 90 minutes every 2 to 4 hours, as needed. do not scratch the spots. for children under 2 years of age: use mittens as needed to prevent scratching. spots on the eyelids clean the eyes with water that has been boiled for 1 minute and has fully cooled. apply wet compresses to the eyelids. gently remove loose scabs. use a separate washcloth for each eye and wash used washcloths. spots in the mouth rinse the mouth with cold water, as needed. drink cold fluids. avoid acidic, spicy, salty, hard, or very hot foods. brush teeth with a very soft toothbrush. avoid mouthwash containing alcohol. for young children: use a cup, spoon, or syringe instead of a bottle because sucking increases mouth pain. for older children: use a straw. from age 4: rinse the mouth with a baking soda solution up to 4 times a day. mix 1⁄2 teaspoon of baking soda with 1 cup (250 ml) of water that has been boiled for 1 minute and has fully cooled. use 5 ml each time for children aged 4 to 6 years, 5 to 10 ml for children aged 6 to 12 years, and 15 ml from age 12. apply an anesthetic gel in the mouth before eating. ask a pharmacist for advice. important: anesthetic gel is not recommended for young children. when in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice. to prevent spreading the infection wash hands often. do not share items that have been in contact with the sick person's mouth. stay home during the most contagious period. avoid close contact with other people, especially babies, children, older adults, and people who are immunocompromised. regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects. avoid scratching the skin sun exposure,
