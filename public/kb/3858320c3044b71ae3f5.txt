stuffy nose official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/stuffy-nose/ nasal congestion what it is official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/stuffy-nose/ a stuffy nose makes breathing through the nose more difficult, either because the lining inside is swollen or because the nose is filled with mucus (secretions). it is often caused by a cold or an infection. what you can do at home official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/stuffy-nose/ what to do blow the nose as often as needed. drink water, herbal tea or broth. for young children: offer water, milk, juice or warm broth often. if the nostrils are red and dry, apply a non-medicated ointment, such as petroleum jelly (vaseline). to relieve pain, take acetaminophen (tylenol) or ibuprofen (advil). when in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice. important: ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age. to help make beathing easier take a warm bath or shower. for children: let them play in the bath, or stay with them in the shower. how to relieve a stuffy nose in children and adults use a saline solution purchased at a pharmacy, or make your own by mixing: 240 ml (1 cup or about 8 oz.) of drinking water or boiled water that has been left to cool 2.5 ml (1⁄2 tsp.) of salt to rinse the nose, use a nasal rinse bottle: 118 ml (4 oz.) for children 237ml (8 oz.) for adolescents and adults wash hands. lean over a sink and keep the mouth open while rinsing. gently squeeze the saline solution into one nostril until it drains out through the other nostril. repeat on the other side. blow the nose afterward, if needed. wash the bottle after each use and replace it every 3 months or when it becomes discoloured. how to relieve a stuffy nose in babies and young children rinse the nose about 4 times a day, or more often as needed. use a nasal aspirator to help remove mucus. wash hands before and after cleaning the child's nose. fill an irrigation syringe with 1 to 3 ml of saline solution. for babies under 6 months: the lying-down technique lay the baby on their side and swaddle them. gently insert the saline solution into the upper nostril using the syringe. mucus should drain from the lower nostril, both nostrils or the mouth. turn the baby onto the other side and repeat for the other nostril. for children over 6 months: the seated technique sit the child on an adult's lap and keep their head upright. wrap the child in a large towel to keep them dry and limit movement. hold the child's chin with one hand and gently rinse one nostril with the other while the child leans slightly forward. mucus may drain from either nostril or the mouth. repeat in the other nostril. avoid smoking or vaping places where there is smoke or dust when to seek medical attention official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/stuffy-nose/ for children: the child's nose cannot be cleared at home and they are having difficulty breathing need help? call info-santé at 811, option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time. go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment. make an appointment at your medical clinic or clsc. if you do not have a family doctor, go to gap.soinsvirtuels or call 811, option 3. when to go to the hospital right away official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/stuffy-nose/ rapid changes in the person's condition, such as: unusual fatigue (drowsiness) or difficulty staying awake reduced responsiveness to what is happening around them confusion, agitation or unusual behaviour hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real) reduced appetite or thirst difficulty carrying out normal daily activities severe difficulty breathing: the skin, lips or fingers are blue or bluish (cyanosis) breathing becomes increasingly difficult, even at rest there is a high-pitched or wheezing sound when breathing (stridor) the chest or abdomen pulls inward with each breath (retractions) in babies and despite drinking plenty of fluids: dry mouth extreme thirst dark yellow urine (pee) no urine (pee) for more than 12 hours crying without tears weakness pale, cold skin, or skin that is slow to return
