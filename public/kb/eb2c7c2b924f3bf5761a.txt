source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/screening-and-carrier-testing-offer/cervical-cancer-screening/information-cervical-cancer-screening persistent human papillomavirus (hpv) infections cause almost all cervical cancers. these very common infections are contracted during sexual contact (including any contact between the genitals or between the genitals and the anus, with or without penetration). hpv vaccination and cervical cancer screening are the two most effective ways to prevent this cancer. cervical cancer screening aims to prevent this cancer and to reduce the mortality and impacts attributable to it. this screening detects cancer precursors (pre-cancerous lesions) or early-stage cancer, before the appearance of symptoms, which increases the chance of recovery. difference between a pap test and an hpv test official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/screening-and-carrier-testing-offer/cervical-cancer-screening/information-cervical-cancer-screening two tests are used in québec for cervical cancer screening: the papanicolaou test (pap test) and the hpv test. the pap test aims to detect abnormal cells and pre-cancerous lesions on the uterine cervix. the hpv test aims to detect the presence of human papillomavirus (hpv). hpv infections occur before the appearance of abnormal cells or pre-cancerous lesions. when a test is used as the main screening test (pap test or hpv test), it is referred to as a primary screening test. the pap test was the primary screening test for cervical cancer in québec until 2023. following a recommendation by the institut national d'excellence en santé et en services sociaux (inesss) in 2022, the replacement of the pap test by the hpv test as the primary screening test for this type of cancer began in 2023 and is ongoing in the various regions. however, the pap test may continue to be used as a complementary test (co-test) in some particular clinical situations. while the primary hpv test is being implemented across québec, the pap test will remain a valid screening test recommended for cervical cancer screening. age to start and to stop getting screening tests pap test as the primary screening test this screening test is offered from the age of 21 years until 65 years. primary hpv test this screening test is offered from the age of 25 years until 65 years. starting hpv screening at 25 years helps maximize the advantages of screening and reduce its disadvantages for participants. before 25 years of age, the hpv screening test has more disadvantages than advantages for the following reasons: most hpv infections resolve on their own, without causing either symptoms or cancer in people younger than 25 years even if an hpv infection were detected, no treatment would be necessary the detection of transient infections has negative effects such as: the stress caused by receiving an abnormal result unnecessary medical appointments additional tests and unnecessary procedures complications from these tests leading to avoidable procedures. interval between screening tests pap test as primary screening test a pap test is recommended every two to three years. primary hpv test a primary hpv test is recommended every five years. adhering to the five-year interval between hpv screening tests maximizes the advantages of screening and reduces its disadvantages. the five-year interval was established based on the specific characteristics of the hpv test and the long delay between hpv infection and cancer development, which can be from 15 to 20 years. transition between the pap test and the hpv test as the primary screening test official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/screening-and-carrier-testing-offer/cervical-cancer-screening/information-cervical-cancer-screening an interval of three years is recommended between your most recent pap test and the next hpv test. it is not recommended to have an hpv test immediately if your most recent screening test took place less than three years ago. for people who are immunocompromised, an interval of one year is recommended between their most recent pap test and their next hpv test. screening earlier than recommended or more frequently could cause more harm to participants, including risks such as: unnecessary follow-up overdiagnosis overtreatment potential complications additional stress or anxiety to find out more, see the advantages, disadvantages and
