fatigue (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fatigue/) official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fatigue/ weakness what it is fatigue is a feeling of low energy that can make everyday activities more difficult. it may be harder to stay active or concentrate. fatigue can be caused by illness, medical treatment, lack of sleep, stress, or a busy lifestyle. what you can do at home what to do rest between activities. plan activities for the times of day when you have the most energy. stop and rest before becoming overly tired. organize your space to reduce unnecessary effort and movement. to help you rest keep your surroundings quiet, comfortable, and at a suitable temperature. avoid noise and other distractions during rest periods. maintain a regular sleep routine. choose relaxing activities such as reading, listening to calming music, or taking a warm bath. healthy lifestyle habits eat regularly throughout the day. drink enough water. do light to moderate physical activity, preferably in the morning or early afternoon. when to seek medical attention persistent sadness, irritability, or loss of interest in usual activities frequent dizziness or problems with balance fatigue that continues despite rest or after following the recommended advice rapid weight loss with no clear cause ongoing difficulty sleeping, despite efforts to improve sleep habits difficulty carrying out everyday activities for children and teenagers: difficulty playing, concentrating, participating in school, or following usual routines need help? call info-santé at 811, option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time. go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment. make an appointment at your medical clinic or clsc. if you do not have a family doctor, go gap.soinsvirtuels or call 811, option 3. when to go to the hospital right away sudden weakness in an arm, leg, or one side of the body severe chest pain difficulty breathing that does not improve, especially if: breathing becomes increasingly difficult, even at rest there is a high-pitched breathing sound (stridor) the chest or abdomen pulls inward with each breath (retractions) unexplained loss of consciousness sudden severe weakness or extreme fatigue that is much worse than usual
