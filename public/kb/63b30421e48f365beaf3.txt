rubella (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/rubella/) official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/rubella/ what it is rubella is a viral infection that may not cause any symptoms. when symptoms do occur, they may include a mild fever, red eyes, and small red spots on the skin. the illness is usually mild, but it can be dangerous during pregnancy. what you can do at home what to do rest. choose calm activities. drink plenty of water. eat according to appetite. have smaller meals more often, if needed. take acetaminophen (tylenol) or ibuprofen (advil). when in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice. important: ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age. to prevent spreading the infection wash hands often. regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects. avoid contact with other people, especially pregnant people. what to avoid touching the nose or mouth of a contagious person sharing drinking glasses or utensils with a contagious person when to seek medical attention for a person aged 6 months or older vaccination may be recommended within 3 days of contact with someone who has rubella for a pregnant person after contact with someone who has rubella the measles, mumps, rubella and chickenpox (varicella) vaccine has not been received or is not up to date make an appointment through clic santé if vaccination is needed need help? call info-santé at 811, option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time. go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment. make an appointment at your medical clinic or clsc. if you do not have a family doctor, go to gap.soinsvirtuels or call 811, option 3. when to go to the hospital right away rapid changes in the person's condition: unusual fatigue (drowsiness) or difficulty staying awake reduced responsiveness to what is happening around them confusion, agitation, or unusual behaviour hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real) reduced appetite or thirst difficulty carrying out normal daily activities
