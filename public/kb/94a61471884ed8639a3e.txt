source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/heat-related-illness-when-body-overheats/ during extreme heat or intense physical activity in a hot environment, the body may have difficulty cooling itself. when the body becomes too hot, symptoms may include headache, nausea, diarrhea (loose, watery stools), or cramps in the legs, arms, or abdomen. heat stroke is the most severe form of heat-related illness. it can develop quickly and become life-threatening if left untreated. body temperature rises to a dangerously high level, and the person may become confused, disoriented, unconscious, or experience seizures (uncontrolled shaking of the body). it is important to go to the hospital immediately if you notice any of these symptoms. heat stroke: a medical emergency official in the shade). apply cool, non-icy water to the entire body. apply ice packs to the neck, armpits, and groin. if cramps occur, loosen clothing and gently massage the affected muscle. staying hydrated drink cool water frequently in small amounts, unless advised otherwise by a healthcare professional. use an electrolyte replacement drink from the pharmacy (such as pedialyte or gastrolyte), or make your own: 360 ml unsweetened orange juice 600 ml cooled boiled water 2.5 ml (1⁄2 tsp) salt drink lightly salted beverages, such as sports drinks (gatorade, powerade). what to avoid avoid intense
