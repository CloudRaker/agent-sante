https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/screening-and-carrier-testing-offer/hpv-test the human papillomavirus screening test (hpv test) helps detect cervical cancer. it detects the presence of high-risk hpv, which can lead to the development of precancerous lesions, followed by cervical cancer. having an hpv test is a personal choice. it is important to learn the advantages, disadvantages, and limits of this screening so that you have all the information you need to make an informed decision whether to take the test or not (see a list of the advantages, disadvantages and limits below). screening frequency and eligible persons official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/screening-and-carrier-testing-offer/hpv-test in the regions where it is offered, hpv test screening is recommended every five years (every three years for immunocompromised individuals) for persons who meet the following criteria: be a woman or a person with a cervix are between 25 and 65 years old not showing any symptoms, which could be associated with cervical cancer (see below) be insured by the québec public health insurance plan health professionals recommend to people over 65 years old that they continue or stop screening depending on their particular situation and the results of their most recent screening tests. those who are not eligible are encouraged to read about the measures for preventing the spread of hpv and other sexually transmitted and blood-borne infections (stbbis). people who show symptoms that could be associated with cervical cancer (e.g., vaginal bleeding, especially after intercourse, or abnormal vaginal discharge) are not part of the target population for this screening. a screening test does not allow a diagnosis to be made. those affected should contact a health professional and undergo a medical follow-up appropriate to their situation. transition between the pap test and the hpv test a delay of three years is recommended between the most recent pap test screening and the next screening with the hpv test. it is not recommended that you undergo hpv test screening right away if your most recent screening was less than three years ago. for immunocompromised persons, a delay of one year is recommended between the most recent pap test screening and the next hpv test screening. screening before these time frames or more frequently than recommended would cause more harm to participants, such as: unnecessary follow-up overdiagnosis overtreatment potential complications additional stress or anxiety for more information, see the advantages, disadvantages and limits of screening. screening test procedure official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/screening-and-carrier-testing-offer/hpv-test pap test representation the screening must be conducted by a health professional. it only takes a few minutes. the health professional first inserts a speculum in the vagina to hold the vaginal walls apart. this makes it easier for the health professional to see and reach the cervix at the upper end of the vagina. using a small brush, the health professional lightly swabs the surface of the cervix to take some cells. these cells are placed in a tube filled with a liquid. the sample is then sent to the laboratory for analysis. when the hpv test is positive (in about 10% of cases), a more detailed analysis of the sample cells is needed. the same sample can be used for the hpv test and the follow-up test on the cells. people therefore do not need to retake the test at this stage. once the results are received, the health professional provides the appropriate follow-up (see the screening results section). advantages, disadvantages and limits of screening official source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/advice-and-prevention/screening-and-carrier-testing-offer/hpv-test it is important to learn the advantages, disadvantages, and limits of screening so that you have all the information you need to make a decision. having the screening done is a personal choice. each person can make an informed decision on whether to undergo screening, according to their values and preferences. it is normal for some people to accept, while others refuse or wait to undergo screening. here are a few things to think about while making your decision. you can talk to a health professional about them, if needed. advantages people aged 25 to 65 years old who have an hpv test every five years can: reduce the risk of developing cervical cancer reduce the risk of dying from cervical cancer because it allows for the detection of abnormal cells or cancers at an early stage. if necessary, it is then possible to remove the affected part of the cervix disadvantages however, there are some disadvantages for the people aged 25 to 65 years old who have an hpv test every five years: waiting periods and worry, for example, when additional tests are needed to confirm the screening results, such as a colposcopy. however, most of the time the results of the colposcopy are normal discomfort (ex., insertion of the speculum) or light bleeding during screening, and sometimes pain caused by additional tests (ex., colposcopy) possibility of overdiagnosis, i.e. the discovery of a cancer that never would have been detected without the screening, and that never would have had an impact on the person's health or life. for example, a cancer that develops very slowly or an indolent cancer. since it is not yet possible to distinguish indolent cancers from fatal cancers, they are all treated. therefore a person who undergoes screening might: have to live with a cancer diagnosis have frequent medical appointments to ensure that the cancer does not reappear feel anxiety about this cancer possible overtreatment, for example, a person who undergoes screening might: receive treatments that might not have been necessar experience side effects of additional tests and treatments the colposcopy can reveal the presence of abnormal cells. these cells could develop into cancer, but not necessarily. since it is impossible to know in advance, the part of the cervix containing abnormal cells must be removed as a precaution. some people may therefore have part of their cervix removed unnecessarily. this situation most often affects younger people. removing part of the cervix can increase the risk of premature birth. for example, a person who has undergone this procedure might give birth before the 34th week in subsequent pregnancies, when a full-term birth is between 37 and 42 weeks. one in every 100 people who have had this procedure is at risk of premature birth. limits of screening participating in cervical cancer screening does not guarantee that: all cervical cancers will be detected all people who have this cancer will survive it some of the people who receive a normal result on the screening test or on additional tests will develop cancer anyway. this situation can arise if: the cancer was invisible or was not detected the cancer had not yet developed at the time of screening it is impossible to know, at the time of screening, exactly who will experience advantages or disadvantages. having an hpv test official source: in your medical file in the institution where the test was performed. it should be noted that a positive hpv test result may be the result of contact with hpv many years ago. approximately 80% of sexually active people will contract an hpv infection during their lifetime. hpv can remain dormant for 15 to 20 years without affecting a person's health or causing symptoms. it is therefore often difficult to know when a person became infected. a person may also have had a negative pap test and later test positive for hpv. this is possible even if their sexual habits have not changed, as the two tests do not detect the same types of changes. in most cases, the immune system clears hpv infections within a few months to a few years. for more information, see the pages human papillomavirus
