https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-issues/a-z/health-problems-associated-with-bathing-waters bathing waters include waters in natural environments (beaches, lakes, rivers) and artificial pools such as indoor and outdoor pools and spas. all bathing waters may contain microorganisms (blue-green algae and other bacteria, viruses, parasites) or other pollutants, depending on the bathing spot. people can also contaminate bathing waters with their secretions, urine, feces or products, such as sunscreen. chlorine is used for disinfection of water in pools and spas to prevent contamination with disease-causing microorganisms. health problems associated with bathing waters mainly appear when a person swallows contaminated water. some microbes can cause gastroenteritis. health problems can also be caused: when the body comes into contact with contaminated water. this can cause infections or irritation when a person breathes in contaminants in the surrounding air in pools and spas bathers are the main source of microbes and organic matter in the water (feces, sweat, urine, skin products, such as cream). to avoid microbial diseases, the water in pools and spas must be filtered and disinfected with chlorine (or bromine). the water quality of public pools and spas must be tested and meet standards to protect health. chlorine can react with organic matter in the water and produce toxic substances called "chloramines". chloramines spread in the air and cause the characteristic chlorine smell of indoor pools. a large quantity of chloramines can cause irritation (eyes, skin or airways). legionella bacteria can grow in poorly maintained spas, end up in the air and cause legionellosis (lung infection). in natural environments (beaches, rivers, lakes) there are many potential sources of pollution by microbes or other contaminants. the quality of the water is often unknown and can change quickly. rain events can alter water quality for at least the next 24 hours. be vigilant when bathing and doing certain water activities. more cases of drowning occur in natural environments. in addition, aquatic activities in natural environments can cause: swimmer's itch
