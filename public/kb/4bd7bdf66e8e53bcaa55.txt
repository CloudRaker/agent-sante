vaccination is the best protection against tetanus, diphtheria, and their complications. vaccination against tetanus and diphteria is recommended for teens aged 14 to 16. two vaccines can be used: combined tetanus and diphteria vaccine (td vaccine) combined tetanus, diphteria, pertussis (whooping cough) vaccine (tdap vaccine) it is safe to receive the tdap vaccine even though the whooping cough component is not required. possible reactions after vaccination are the same for both vaccines. diseases signs and symptoms possible complications tetanus muscle spasms in the jaw<br>vocal chord https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/fiches_vaccins/25-278-06a.pdf vaccination is the best protection against tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis (whooping cough) and their complications. the td and tdap vaccines can be given to people aged 4 and older who require protection against these diseases. the td or tdap vaccine is usually given in secondary 3. some symptoms may be caused by the vaccine (e.g. redness at the injection site). other problems may occur by chance and are not related to the vaccine (e.g. cold, gastro, headache). the td and tdap vaccines are safe. most reactions are harmless and do not last long. frequency possible reactions to these vaccines what to do in most cases<br>(more than 50% of people) pain at the injection site<br>fatigue, headache very often<br>(less sterile abscess at the injection site we recommend that you stay at the clinic for at least 15 minutes after vaccination because allergic reactions may occur. if an allergic reaction occurs, the symptoms will appear a few minutes after the vaccination. the person giving the vaccine will be able to treat this reaction immediately. for any questions, contact the person giving the vaccine, or
