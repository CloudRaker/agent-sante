https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/bruise-or-bump-no-open-wound/ a bruise or bump can happen after a hit or fall. there is no open wound, but the tissue underneath is injured. if the injury is minor, a small bruise may appear. if the injury is more severe, there may be a larger bump or bleeding under the skin. for a few days, there may be pain, swelling, redness, or warmth. these symptoms usually start to improve after 3 days. what you can do at home official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/bruise-or-bump-no-open-wound/ what to do keep the injured area elevated, unless it is deformed (possible fracture) or unless the injury involves the neck area. to reduce pain, take acetaminophen (tylenol) or ibuprofen (advil). when in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice. apply a cold compress cold helps reduce swelling and pain. during the first 48 hours (2 days) after the injury, apply a cold compress for 15 to 20 minutes every 2 hours. use a cold compress, ice pack, bag of frozen vegetables, or gel ice pack. always place a cloth between the skin and the cold source to protect the skin. do not apply a cold compress if you have a problem with circulation. apply heat starting on the third day, apply heat for 20 to 30 minutes, 4 times a day. use a damp towel warmed in the microwave, a warm compress, or a hot water bottle. always place a cloth between the skin and the heat source to protect the skin. do not apply heat if you have problems with circulations or a blood clotting disorder. do not apply heat when using a pain-relief (analgesic) gel. what to avoid if the injured area is deformed (possible fracture), do not move it. if the injury involves the neck area, do not move the neck or the injured person. do not massage the injured area. when to seek medical attention official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/bruise-or-bump-no-open-wound/ difficulty moving or walking large bruise (significant collection of blood or hematoma) on the ear signs of infection after 72 hours (3 days), even after following the recommended care: increasing pain skin around the injury becoming red or swollen, or pus (yellow-green fluid) appearing need help? call info-santé at 811, option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time. go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment. make an appointment at your medical clinic or clsc. if you do not
