or her against rotavirus gastroenteritis and its complications. before vaccination, over 95% of children under the age of 5 were affected by rotavirus gastroenteritis. disease signs and symptoms possible complications rotavirus<br>gastroenteritis diarrhea<br>vomiting<br>fever dehydration<br>hospitalization to treat dehydration (in québec,<br>900 to 1,500 hospitalizations per year before vaccine<br>arrival) important it's up to you to make an appointment with the clsc or with your doctor to have your child vaccinated. next appointment for vaccination: information on the rotavirus vaccine vaccination is the best protection against rotavirus gastroenteritis and its complications. the 1st dose should be given before the age of 20 weeks, and the 2nd one before the age of 8 months. because the virus contained in the vaccine may be found in stools for about 10 days after vaccination, it is important to wash your hands carefully when changing diapers. some symptoms may be caused by the vaccine. other problems may occur by chance and are not related to the vaccine (e.g. cold, gastro, headache). rotavirus vaccine is safe. in most cases, it does not cause any reaction. frequency known reactions to this vaccine what to do often<br>(less than 10% of children) diarrhea or vomiting use a medication for fever or very rarely<br>(1 to 7 children in 100,000) during the week following vaccination, there is a possible risk<br>of intestinal intussusception, in other words a form of obstruction<br>in the intestines requiring hospital treatment. discomfort if needed.<br>see a doctor if symptoms are severe. we recommend that you stay at the clinic for at least 15 minutes after vaccination because allergic reactions may occur. if an allergic reaction occurs, the symptoms will appear a few minutes after the vaccination. the person giving the vaccine will be able to treat this reaction immediately. for any questions, contact the person giving the vaccine, or info-santé 8-1-1 or your doctor. you can also consult québec.ca/vaccination
