impetigo (https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/impetigo/) official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/impetigo/ what it is impetigo is a bacterial skin infection that causes clusters of small red sores, often covered with a yellow crust. impetigo spreads easily, especially in childcare settings, and is more common in warm, humid weather. what you can do at home what to do soak the sores in soapy water at least twice a day. remove any crusts that come off easily. do not pull off crusts that are still attached to the sore. avoid scratching. if an antibiotic cream is prescribed follow the healthcare professional's instructions. apply the antibiotic cream to the sores. cover the sores with a dry bandage until they have dried. to prevent spreading the infection wash hands often. do not share towels or washcloths. regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects. cough and sneeze into the elbow or a tissue. when to seek medical attention the sores get larger 48 hours after treatment begins red streaks extend from the sores a blister or sore is more than 2.5 cm wide the skin around a sore becomes painful, red, swollen, or warm, or contains pus (yellow-green fluid) the affected person has eczema (atopic dermatitis) or is immunocompromised for children under 1 year of age: consulter si des symptômes apparaissent après un contact avec une personne atteinte need help? call info-santé at 811, option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time. go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment. make an appointment at your medical clinic or clsc. if you do not have a family doctor, go to gap.soinsvirtuels or call 811, option 3. when to go to the hospital right away red streaks extending from the sores, along with: signs of an infection that is getting worse quickly such as skin that becomes red, swollen, or very painful fever, chills or shaking rapid heartbeat, or palpitations rapid changes in general condition, such as: unusual fatigue or difficulty staying awake reduced responsiveness confusion, agitation or unusual behaviour hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not real) reduced appetite or thirst difficulty carrying out normal daily activities
