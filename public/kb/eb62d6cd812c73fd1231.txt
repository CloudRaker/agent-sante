constipation - ages 0-11 official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-0-11/ what it is official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-0-11/ constipation is when it is hard to have a bowel movement (poop) or when bowel movements happen less often than usual. stools (poop) may be hard. there may be stomach pain or a feeling of still needing to go, even after a bowel movement. constipation is not a cause for concern, but it is important to find a way to get relief. related sheet available: constipation - ages 12 and over what you can do at home official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-0-11/ what to do for babies under 6 months do not give water or any liquids other than breast milk or infant formula. make sure the baby drinks enough milk for their age. if the baby is formula-fed: follow the preparation instructions on the formula package. choose a formula best suited to the baby's age. for babies over 6 months who eat solid foods offer water every day, in addition to milk. offer high-fiber foods, such as: vegetables; fruit (prunes, pears, and apples); whole-grain breads and cereals; legumes. children ages 1 to 11 offer high-fiber foods at least 3 times a day, such as fruit (bananas, pears, figs, plums), vegetables, whole-grain breads and cereals, and legumes. add 1 teaspoon of wheat bran to cereal or yogurt. serve small meals. encourage the child to chew food thoroughly. offer mainly water to drink. offer apple, pear, or prune juice. important: some foods, such as dried fruit, can be a choking hazard depending on the child's age. cut them into small pieces or prepare them so they are safe for the child to eat. making bowel movements easier during toilet training: offer a potty rather than the toilet. every day, encourage the child to sit on the potty at the same times for 5 to 10 minutes, especially after meals (toileting schedule). let the child watch a parent use the toilet. comfort the child if painful bowel movements are making toilet training harder. if needed, take a temporary break from toilet training. if the child is toilet trained: every day, encourage the child to sit on the toilet at the same times for 5 to 10 minutes, especially after meals (toileting schedule). provide a small stool for the child to rest their feet on. gently massage the abdomen in a clockwise direction 3 to 4 times a day. encourage the child to be active during playtime. avoid do not use medications to help with bowel movements (laxatives) without medical advice. ask a pharmacist for advice before using any constipation treatment. when to seek medical attention official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-0-11/ constipation that keeps coming back difficulty passing stools for babies under 1 year: fever and no bowel movements stools that are often small, watery, or hard fewer than 2 bowel movements a day in breastfed babies under 4 weeks old for babies and children under 11: constipation (no bowel movements) for several days frequent pain when having a bowel movement ribbon-shaped stools for children ages 1 to 11: red blood in the stool (even a small amount) refusing to eat not gaining enough weight for children ages 4 and over: passing stool into their underwear without meaning to (involuntary leakage) need help? call info-santé at 811, option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time. go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment. make an appointment at your medical clinic or clsc. if you do not have a family doctor, go to gap.soinsvirtuels or call 811, option 3. when to go to the hospital right away official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/constipation-ages-0-11/ black blood or a lot activities for babies under 3 months of age: fever for babies under 1 year: stomach that is very painful to the touch continuous crying that cannot be soothed swollen stomach with frequent vomiting
