or sting occurs when an insect (such as a tick, mosquito, wasp, or bee) pierces the skin and injects venom or saliva. it can cause pain, redness, swelling, or itching. most bites and stings are call the poison control centre: 1- 800- 463- 5060. removing a stinger remove the stinger promptly by gently scraping the skin. wash the area with soap and water. tick bites using tweezers, grasp the tick close to the skin and pull gently. avoid crushing the tick during removal. place the tick in a small, sealed container. record the date, time, and location of the bite. wash the area with soap and water. call info-santé 811, option 1, to report the tick bite. relieving pain apply ice for 10 minutes every 30 minutes for 4 hours. wrap the ice in a cloth to prevent skin damage. if swelling is present, remove jewelry, rings, or bracelets near the bite or sting. elevate the affected arm or leg above heart level. take acetaminophen (tylenol) or ibuprofen (advil). if in doubt, consult a pharmacist. important: ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age. relieving itching avoid scratching, even if the area is itchy. apply a cold washcloth or a paste made of equal parts water and baking soda. leave it on for a few seconds while applying gentle pressure. ask a pharmacist whether over-the-counter medications or creams may help. when to seek medical attention official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/insect-bite-or-sting/ if you have any of these signs: a tick cannot be removed completely. pain, itching, or swelling persists despite following these recommendations. the skin around the bite or sting becomes red, swollen, warm, or develops pus (yellow-green fluid). need help? call info-santé at 811, option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time. go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment. make an appointment at your medical clinic or clsc. if you do not have a family doctor, go to gap.soinsvirtuels or call 811, option 3.
