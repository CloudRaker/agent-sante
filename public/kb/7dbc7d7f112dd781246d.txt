fifth disease official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fifth-disease/ erythema infectiosum what it is official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fifth-disease/ fifth disease, also known as erythema infectiosum, is a viral infection. it occurs mainly in winter and spring and is usually harmless. symptoms may include: red cheeks, fatigue, headache, a mild fever. a lace-like rash may also develop on the body. what you can do at home official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fifth-disease/ what to do get as much rest as possible. to relieve pain or fever, take acetaminophen (tylenol) or ibuprofen (advil). when in doubt, ask a pharmacist for advice. important: ibuprofen is contraindicated for children under 6 months of age. if needed, see the health advice sheets on fever - ages 0 to 11 fever - ages 12 and older if the skin is itchy avoid scratching. wear loose, soft clothing. apply cold-water compresses to the skin for 30 to 90 minutes every 2 to 4 hours, as needed. use a mild, unscented soap. moisturize the skin with cream unless this makes the rash worse. for children under 2 years of age: use mittens as needed to prevent scratching. take a 10- to 30-minute lukewarm bath without soap, bubble bath, or scented products 3 to 4 times a day. if the skin is dry, take only one 10-minute bath a day. if possible, add baking soda to the bathwater: for children under 2: 50 to 60 ml for adults and children aged 2 and older: 250 to 500 ml important: never leave a child unattended in the bath. to prevent spreading the infection wash hands often. cough and sneeze into the elbow or a tissue. avoid contact with: pregnant people; people who are immunocompromised; people with a blood disorder, such as anemia. regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces and objects. avoid sharing food or utensils. quand consulter official source: https://sante.quebec/en/health-advice/fifth-disease/ after contact with someone who has fifth disease, for people at risk, including pregnant people people who are immunocompromised people with a blood disorder such as anemia signs of anemia, such as severe fatigue pale lips a rapid heartbeat shortness of breath during physical activity need help? call info-santé at 811, option 1, to speak with a nurse at any time. go to the pharmacy for advice on medications or treatment. make an appointment at your medical clinic or clsc. if you do not have a family doctor, go to gap.soinsvirtuels or call 811, option 3.
